Jiahao Tian

I am an undergraduate student majoring in Statistics at the School of Mathematical Sciences, Zhejiang University.

My research interests lie in high-dimensional inference, uncertainty quantification, and theoretical statistics. I am particularly interested in developing statistically principled methods that remain reliable under distribution shift, heavy-tailed distributions, and model misspecification.

I am currently working on conformal prediction under covariate shift and robust multiple-knockoff aggregation.

Research

Heavy-Tailed Robustness of Multiple-Knockoff Aggregation Methods

With Prof. Lijun Wang, Zhejiang University

I study the robustness of multiple-knockoff aggregation procedures under heavy-tailed distributions, with particular attention to selection instability and threshold inflation.

More about this project →

Distributional Balancing Weights for Conformal Calibration

With Prof. Guanhua Chen, UW–Madison

I develop distributional balancing methods for conformal prediction under covariate shift, using tools from Fourier analysis, RKHS theory, and empirical process theory.
